What is Market Positioning and How Important is it in Your Business?

By Arjun Bingipur · Published March 2026

The key to standing out in today's competitive business climate is to embrace branding[cite: 9]. Research on brand consistency has repeatedly found that businesses which present their branding consistently across every channel can see revenue increases in the region of 23%, but it is not just about the revenue[cite: 9]. Improved branding means a better market position while improving customer loyalty and reliability[cite: 9]. Branding and market positioning are symbiotic — for those companies that utilise a robust branding strategy combined with a strong focus on improving market positioning, growth is far easier to achieve[cite: 9].

What is Market Positioning?

At its most basic, market positioning is when you firmly establish the identity of your company or product so that it has more immediate recognition and a specific perception[cite: 9]. A clean example would be Mercedes — immediately recognisable as a luxury car[cite: 9]. Having the right market positioning strategies means knowing who your target audience is so that you can target your branding more accurately[cite: 9]. Even small businesses that establish their positioning statement can compete with the largest companies in the world[cite: 9].

Identifying Your Target Market

All market positioning strategies will first require you to learn more about your target audience[cite: 9]. By focusing on a specific target audience, you can aim your marketing at the people who are likely to buy from you or hire your services[cite: 9]. To define your target market[cite: 9]:

  • Existing Customers — identify commonalities and learn what drives them to spend[cite: 9]
  • Competitor Research — look for niches not being served by current brands[cite: 9]
  • Know Your Product — list the best features and brainstorm the audiences who value them[cite: 9]
  • Identifying Demographics — fine-tune by age, geography, income, gender, education and more[cite: 9]

Determining Your Current Market Position

Before you start fine-tuning your branding and marketing, you need to identify exactly what your current market position is[cite: 9]. Your unique selling point (USP) is what sets you apart from your competitors and is the key to your effective branding[cite: 9]. You will need to identify those USPs that attract customers because they want or need what you offer — aim to be the only business that offers that exact solution[cite: 9].

The Importance of Niche Audiences

When consumers do not understand your brand, it can take a lot of time, energy, and marketing budget to change their minds[cite: 9]. The goal of your positioning strategy should be to align the perception of your brand with what you want it to be[cite: 9]. Differentiating yourself from your competitors is crucial — find those niches and customers that are not getting what they need from your competitors and fill those gaps[cite: 9].

Positioning Your Business

Once you have identified your USP and finely tuned your branding, it is time to use those to work on improving your market position[cite: 9]. Your marketing will be more effective because it will be more likely to resonate with your target audience[cite: 9]. Use your customer data to find out where and when your customers spend their time, and focus your efforts on those channels for maximum return[cite: 9]. Positioning work rarely stands alone — it tends to feed directly into the kind of strategic planning that turns a clearer market position into a concrete set of priorities for the year ahead[cite: 9].
